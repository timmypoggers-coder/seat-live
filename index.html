    How it works

    Customers see available seats on buses in real time on this website. When a passenger boards, they scan a registered ticket or card. The passenger is added to that bus's list and the occupied and free seat counts update automatically. With 50 seats and 30 boarded, 20 seats show as free.

    Why it helps

    Commuters can see how full a bus is and decide whether to go to the terminal, instead of arriving to find every bus full. Boarding records show when most passengers board and when few do, so passengers can plan trips and operators can see demand.

    What is new

    Scanning tickets identifies who actually boarded, so there is less reliance on manual counting or conductors updating figures by hand. It also separates boarded passengers from people who only reserved. Digital identification, registration, capacity monitoring and data analysis already exist separately. The invention combines them to improve journey planning.

    This demonstration

    Buses and passengers here are simulated, so no real public bus is needed. QR codes stand in for tickets or cards, a webcam or QR reader can scan them, and a small in-page database holds passengers and buses. The board shows Bus ID, route, total seats, occupied seats and free seats.
